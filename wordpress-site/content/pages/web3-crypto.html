What we fix in onchain products

Onboarding that frightens newcomers off

Seed phrases, gas, and networks presented in the first sixty seconds. We sequence the scary parts after first value, and design recovery that does not depend on a screenshot in someone's camera roll.

Signing requests nobody can read

If the user cannot tell what they are approving, they are gambling. We design transaction previews in plain language: what moves, to whom, what it costs, and what it cannot be undone from.

Brands that trade credibility for hype

Neon gradients and anonymous mascots make a serious protocol look like a rug. We design identities that read as financial infrastructure, because increasingly that is what they are.

What onchain teams need, by stage.

Explain the mechanism

Documentation, a site, and visual explanations that make the protocol legible to developers, investors, and press without resorting to jargon or promises.

Design the irreversible moments

Wallet connection, signing, bridging, and recovery — the four places users lose money. Everything else can be iterated; these have to be right at launch.

Reach beyond crypto-native users

Fiat on-ramps, account abstraction, and language that assumes no prior knowledge, for the audience that will never read a block explorer.

Web3 design questions

How do you design onboarding without scaring people?

Let people do something useful before custody becomes their problem, explain each irreversible step at the moment it happens, and offer recovery options that match how real people store secrets.

How do you make transactions understandable?

Translate the call data into a sentence: what leaves the wallet, what arrives, what it costs, and what permission is being granted indefinitely. Approval scope is where most losses start.

Can you design for both crypto-native and new users?

Yes, by layering: a simple default path with the advanced controls available but not in the way. Trying to serve both in one undifferentiated interface serves neither.

Working with onchain teams

Do you work with pre-launch and stealth protocols?

Yes, under NDA. A good deal of onchain work happens before a token or mainnet launch, and we treat timing as confidential.

Do you design token or marketing material?

We design brand, site, and product. We do not write or design anything that promises returns, and we will push back on claims that read as financial advice.

Can you work with our smart contract constraints?

Yes. We ask your engineers in week one what is on-chain, what is indexed, and what is slow — those constraints shape the interface more than any visual decision.
