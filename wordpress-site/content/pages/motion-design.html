Where motion earns its place

Explaining something a screenshot can't

If your product's value is a process — data moving, a flow completing, a state changing — motion explains it in four seconds where a paragraph takes thirty.

Making the interface feel fast

Transitions that carry context, loading states that stay honest, micro-interactions that confirm an action. Perceived speed is a design decision, not an engineering one.

Launches and fundraising

A launch video, a deck that animates the one slide that matters, social cuts sized for each platform. Produced from the same source files, so the story stays identical everywhere.

Three steps. No surprises.

Agree the point

Every animation has one job: explain a mechanism, carry a transition, or sell a moment. We write that in a sentence and agree the format and performance budget before production.

Storyboard, then animate

You approve timing and key frames as stills before anything is rendered — the stage where changes are cheap. Then full production with reviewable passes.

Deliver production-ready

Lottie, Rive, MP4, or WebM at the sizes your site can afford, with fallbacks and reduced-motion variants. Implementation notes for your developers included.

How long does it take?

Lottie, Rive, or video?

Lottie for lightweight vector animation that scales; Rive when it must respond to user input or state; video when the content is screen capture or needs photographic quality. We recommend per asset.

Performance and accessibility

Will it slow our site down?

Not if the budget is set first. We agree a file-size ceiling per asset, lazy-load below the fold, and measure the result against your Core Web Vitals before handover.

What about reduced motion?

Every delivery includes a reduced-motion variant that respects the user's system setting. It is a requirement in our process, not an upgrade.

Can you animate our existing designs?

Yes. Send the Figma file. If the layers will not survive export we will tell you what needs restructuring before we quote.
