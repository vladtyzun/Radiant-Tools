What we fix in AI products

A blank box and no idea what to type

Open-ended input is the hardest interface there is. We design entry points, examples, and constraints that show people what the product is good at before they waste a prompt discovering what it is not.

Wrong answers handled as edge cases

In a probabilistic product, being wrong is a core state, not an exception. We design correction, citation, and escalation paths so a bad answer costs trust once rather than permanently.

No visible reason to believe it

Sources, confidence, and what the system did on your behalf have to be visible at the moment of the decision. Hiding the working makes a good model feel like a guess.

AI design questions

How do you design trust into an AI product?

Show the source, show the confidence, and make correction cheap. Users forgive a system that is transparent about uncertainty and abandon one that is confidently wrong.

Should our AI feature be a chat interface?

Usually not on its own. Chat is a fallback for intent you cannot predict; most value comes from putting the capability inside the workflow the user is already in.

How do you design something that changes weekly?

Design the frame, not the output: states, boundaries, and review patterns that hold while the model improves underneath them.
