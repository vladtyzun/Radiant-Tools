What we fix in health products

Interfaces that assume a calm user

People use health products while worried, tired, or in pain. We design for low attention and high stakes: plain language, one decision per screen, and no irreversible action without a clear way back.

Consent treated as paperwork

Consent and privacy are the first real conversation you have with a patient. We design them to be read and understood, which is also what regulators increasingly expect.

Clinical tools that cost seconds per click

A clinician repeats a workflow forty times a day. We design around the repetition — defaults, keyboard paths, and dense views that respect how little time there is.

Healthtech design questions

Do you work with regulated medical products?

We design them; we do not certify them. We work alongside your regulatory lead, document design decisions where they matter for a submission, and keep usability evidence where it is required. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm experience with IEC 62366 or FDA human factors if any.]

How do you handle patient data in design work?

We work with synthetic or anonymised data, sign a BAA or equivalent where applicable, and never put real patient records into design tools. [PLACEHOLDER: confirm your standard data agreement.]

Is accessibility mandatory for health products?

Treat it as mandatory. Public-sector and EU-facing health services are explicitly covered, and health products reach people with impairments at a far higher rate than average.

Do you design for devices as well as apps?

We design the software layer around a device — pairing, readings, alerts, and the moments a reading needs a human response.

How long does a health product engagement take?

A patient-facing MVP takes eight to twelve weeks. Clinical tooling takes longer, because the research and validation steps are not compressible.
