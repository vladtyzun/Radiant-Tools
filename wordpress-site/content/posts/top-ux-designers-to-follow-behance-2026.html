If Dribbble is where designers show their best individual shots, Behance is where they explain their thinking. The platform's project format - long-form, sequential, built around a narrative arc from problem to solution - has made it the default home for genuine UX case studies, which. Is exactly why it's the better platform for learning from other designers' process, even if Dribbble remains stronger for quick visual-style browsing.

Why Behance for Process, Specifically

Behance's project format naturally encourages designers to document research findings, iteration history, and the reasoning behind decisions - not because the platform enforces it, but. Because the long-scroll case-study format makes a "just the pretty final screens" project feel visibly incomplete in a way a Dribbble shot grid doesn't. This structural difference is why Behance is the better platform specifically for studying how good UX designers think, not just what their final work looks like.

How to filter any Behance profile before you follow blindly

Prioritize projects that show raw research artifacts, named constraints, failed iterations, and outcomes. Specialty tags that still work for discovery: "UX Research," "Information Architecture," "Design System," "Accessible Design," "SaaS UX," and "UX Writing."

What Separates a Genuinely Instructive Case Study From a Padded One

Not every long-form Behance project is equally valuable to study. Look for: specific, named constraints (real technical or business limitations the designer navigated, not vague "challenges"). visible failed iterations (not just the winning final direction), measurable or at least directionally stated outcomes (what changed after the design shipped), and. a clear point of view about tradeoffs made, rather than a project that presents every decision as obviously correct in hindsight.

FAQ

Is Behance still relevant given how much design content has moved to other platforms? Yes, specifically for long-form case study documentation - no other major platform has replaced its role for that specific format. Even as quick visual inspiration has shifted more toward Dribbble, Mobbin, and Pinterest-style platforms.

Should I evaluate a potential hire's Behance case studies differently than their Dribbble shots? Yes - Behance case studies are better for evaluating process and strategic thinking, while. Dribbble shots are better for quickly evaluating visual craft and style fit.

How do I know if a Behance case study is genuinely instructive or just well-produced marketing for the designer? Look for the specific markers covered above - visible failed iterations, named real constraints, and stated (even if modest) outcomes. Case studies presenting every decision as obviously, uniformly correct are usually more marketing than genuine process documentation.

Are there UX specialties that are hard to find good Behance case studies for? Yes - genuinely novel specialties like agentic UX and AI trust design are still building up a case-study library, since the patterns themselves are new.
